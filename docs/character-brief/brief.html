Soul Jam Character Rig Brief

A realistic, textured basketball player for a real-time 3D web game (three.js, WebGL 2). The character is driven by motion capture on a fixed skeleton, and a physically simulated basketball touches the hands every frame, so the hands must be modelled and rigged with real care.

units metresup +Yfacing +Zskeleton 127 joints (supplied)format GLB (glTF 2.0)ball size 7, r = 0.12 m

01The job

Model, texture, skin and test one game character on the skeleton we supply. The look (face, build, outfit) comes from the client's separate references. This brief covers everything the game needs technically.

The game plays motion-captured animation on this exact skeleton. The mesh, weights and hands you make are what players see catching, dribbling and shooting a physics ball, up close.

02What you get

player-rig-reference.glb

The skeleton, the current body mesh in its bind pose and its skin weights. Import it into Blender (File › Import › glTF 2.0) or Maya (glTF importer). This is your starting point and your scale reference.

skeleton.json

Every joint's name, parent and bind position / rotation (metres, world space, quaternion x y z w).

This brief

Requirements, numbers and the acceptance tests we run on delivery.

The reference body is 1.83 m tall with its feet on Y = 0. Its current fingers are low-detail; replacing them with properly modelled hands is a main goal of this job.

03Non-negotiables

Must
  • Rig to the supplied skeleton: same 127 joints, same names, same hierarchy. Don't rename, add, remove or re-parent joints, and don't substitute a Mixamo, UE5 or HumanIK skeleton.
  • Keep the supplied proportions: each bone within 2 cm of the supplied length. If the character's build needs different proportions (taller, longer arms, bigger hands), tell us before rigging. We refit the skeleton to your mesh and send it back.
  • Metres, +Y up, character faces +Z, feet on Y = 0, centred on the origin. No scale or rotation left on the root or mesh objects.
  • At most 4 bone influences per vertex, normalised, and no unweighted vertices. No weight on the end markers (joints ending in _null).
  • Deliver GLB with embedded textures, plus your source file and texture sources.

Why so strict: the animation data are joint rotations for this skeleton, captured from real players. Any other joint layout breaks every animation in the game.

Bind pose

  • Preferred: the supplied pose: arms about 15° out from the body, elbows slightly bent (about 12°), legs about 6° apart.
  • Also accepted: an A-pose or T-pose on the same skeleton, if that gives you cleaner shoulders. Our importer re-poses the mesh through your own skin weights, so keep those weights clean in the pose you bind in.

Twist joints

Weight the upper arm, forearm, thigh and shin twist joints in a smooth gradient along each limb. A wrist that turns 90° must not candy-wrap the forearm.

07Hands

This is the most important part of the job. The game keeps a physical ball on the palm and wraps each fingertip onto the ball's surface. What you model is exactly what touches the ball.

Knuckle width (index to pinky base) is 6.5 cm, and the hand is 17.8 cm long from wrist to middle fingertip. If the character needs bigger hands, say so before rigging and we'll refit the skeleton.

Modelling

  • Fully separate fingers with natural webbing, and fingernails modelled as geometry.
  • Knuckle and crease detail in the normal map, plus palm pads with a slight natural cup.
  • Finger thickness close to the game's contact sizes, within ±20%: about 1.9 cm across the index to ring fingers, about 2.2 cm for the thumb, and a 1.4 cm thick palm pad over the bones. If the visible hand is much thicker or thinner, the ball will visibly float or sink into it.
  • Palm centre between the wrist joint and the middle-finger knuckle. The ball sits there during a dribble.

Rigging

  • Each finger segment moves rigidly with its own joint. Weights blend only across the joint crease, over about 2 edge loops.
  • No weight bleeding between neighbouring fingers.
  • thumb0 and thumb1 drive the thumb's base muscle, so it bulges and flattens believably.
  • pinky0 cups the outer palm.
  • A full fist must close without any finger collapsing or poking through another.

Hand pose tests (render each, and include the poses in your source file)

  1. Open palm: fingers straight and together, palm flat.
  2. Fist: all fingers fully curled, thumb over the middle segments.
  3. Ball grip: a 0.12 m radius sphere (a size 7 basketball) held in the right hand, every fingertip on its surface, nothing passing into it.
  4. Dribble push: fingers spread over the top of the ball, palm slightly cupped.
  5. Shooting hand: ball on the finger pads, wrist cocked back about 70°, guide hand on the side of the ball.
  6. Two-hand gather: both palms on opposite sides of the ball, chest height.

09Face & eyes

  • Neutral expression, mouth closed.
  • Eyes as separate meshes, each weighted 100% to l_eye / r_eye. Lower jaw and chin weighted to c_jaw.
  • Blendshapes aren't required. If you offer the ARKit 52 set, quote it as a separate optional extra.

10Delivery & acceptance

Deliver

  • character.glb: glTF 2.0 binary with embedded textures, rigged to the supplied skeleton, under 25 MB.
  • Source file: .blend, or .ma / .mb, with the hand test poses as keyframes or pose-library entries.
  • Texture sources (PSD / Substance / 4K PNG).
  • Renders: front, side and back turnaround, the 6 hand poses, and the deformation poses.

How we accept it

  1. Our import tool reads the GLB and reports no warnings: all joints matched by name, bone lengths within 2 cm, at most 4 influences per vertex.
  2. The bind pose imports clean: feet on the floor, nothing inverted, textures the right way up.
  3. In the game, a range-of-motion clip plays without the deformation problems listed above.
  4. In the game, the dribble, crossover and shot animations play with the physics ball. Fingers meet the ball's surface without visible gaps or passing into it.

Questions about joints, pose or scale are always welcome before you start. It's much cheaper to adjust the skeleton to your model up front than to redo skin weights later.
